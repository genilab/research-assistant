# Academic Writing Guidelines

Apply these rules to every topic publication, paper analysis, review, and
experiment report. A paper-type skill may add structure but may not weaken the
evidence or uncertainty rules below.

## Evidence and claims

- Link substantive factual claims to a topic reference, paper record, result,
  or clearly named supplied source.
- Separate reported findings, interpretation, proposal, and open question.
- State scope conditions, conflicting evidence, and limits where they affect a
  conclusion. Never turn a plausible explanation into an established result.

## Clear scholarly prose

- State the subject and claim plainly. Prefer active voice when the actor
  matters and define terms when ambiguity is likely.
- Use concrete descriptions, measured quantities, and named sources instead of
  generic claims about importance, novelty, robustness, or consensus.
- Remove stock LLM phrases, filler transitions, rhetorical questions,
  promotional language, forced lists, and unsupported claims of completeness.
- Give each paragraph one controlling idea. Preserve qualified claims rather
  than simplifying away their conditions.

## Paper controls

Start a publication with title, topic, type, status, date, audience, and its
central question or claim. Use one H1 title and concise H2 sections. Add a
dated entry to the topic publication index. Before marking a draft reviewed,
check sources, required sections, counterevidence, limitations, and index links.

## Learning from examples

Use only researcher-supplied papers or instructions to infer a writing
convention. Record a concise, reusable pattern and its evidence in this file
through `writing-refine`; do not copy a paper's prose or assume that one
example establishes a universal convention.
