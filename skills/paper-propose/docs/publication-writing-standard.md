# Publication Writing Guidelines

These guidelines apply to every paper, analysis, and report in a topic's
`publications/` folder. Apply them together with the
[Academic Writing Guidelines](../../writing-refine/docs/scholarly-writing-standard.md).
A paper-type skill may add required sections, but it must preserve these shared
rules.

## Document controls

Start every publication with a concise document-control block: title, topic,
document type, status (`outline`, `draft`, or `reviewed`), date, intended
audience, and central question or claim. Use a descriptive, collision-safe
filename and add one dated entry to the topic's `publications/INFO.md`.

## Evidence and claims

- Link factual claims to the topic's `{topic-slug}_refs.md` entries or clearly
  identify supplied source material. Never invent citations, quotations, data,
  consensus, or a source's conclusions.
- Separate sourced findings, interpretation, proposal, and open question in
  the prose. State material uncertainty, conflict, scope limits, and evidence
  gaps where they affect the argument.
- Use the topic's info record for established or attributed learning and its
  ideas record for hypotheses or next questions; a publication may discuss
  either only with its status made clear.

## Common paper shape

Use one H1 title, concise H2 sections, and informative H3 subsections only
when needed. Unless a paper-type structure says otherwise, include: an
abstract or executive summary, problem/context, central question or thesis,
evidence and analysis, limitations, implications or next steps, and
references. Write for the declared audience, favor precise claims over broad
assertions, and preserve the citation style already used in the topic.

## Review before marking a draft reviewed

Check that the title and type match the file, every substantive external claim
has a traceable source or an uncertainty label, required paper-type sections
are present, counterevidence is represented where material, and the local
publication index is current.
